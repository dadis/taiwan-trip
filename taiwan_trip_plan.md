## 3. Accommodation Neighborhood Guide (Where to Book)

### 1. Hualien (2 nights: Nov 16–18)
- **Top Pick:** **Hualien Golden Triangle / City Center** (around Zhongshan & Zhongzheng Roads).
  - *Why:* Walkable to all the best local food spots, bakeries, cafes, and a pleasant 10–15 min stroll to Dongdamen Night Market.
  - *Alternative:* **Near Hualien Railway Station** (ideal if you want zero hassle with luggage upon arrival/departure).

### 2. Taitung (3 nights: Nov 18–21)
- **Top Pick:** **Taitung City (near Tiehua Music Village / Old Railway Walkway)**.
  - *Why:* The cultural heartbeat of Taitung. At night, illuminated lanterns line the park with acoustic live music, craft beer stalls, and cafes. Very relaxed and safe.
  - *Day trips:* Take a quick 40-minute scenic train up to Chishang for cycling without needing to pack and haul your bags.

### 3. Kaohsiung (2 nights: Nov 21–23)
- **Top Pick:** **Yancheng District (near Pier-2 Art Center or MRT Yanchengpu)**.
  - *Why:* Best artistic vibe in Taiwan. Historic warehouse district right on the harbor, filled with boutique cafes, microbreweries, and street art. Direct access to the Light Rail and the Cijin Island ferry.

### 4. Tainan (3 nights: Nov 23–26)
- **Top Pick:** **West Central District (Zhongxi District)** — near Shennong Street, Chihkan Tower, or Tainan Art Museum.
  - *Why:* Tainan does not have an MRT subway system (it relies on walking and short taxis), so staying in the West Central District is essential. You step out of your boutique hotel/B&B directly into winding alleys, temple squares, and legendary food stalls.

### 5. Taipei Finale (3 nights: Nov 26–29)
- **Top Pick:** **Zhongshan District (near MRT Zhongshan Station)**.
  - *Why:* The epicenter of modern Taipei cafe and design culture (Chifeng Street). Directly on the Red MRT line (which takes you straight to Beitou Hot Springs, Taipei Main Station, and Taipei 101), and only 1 stop from the Airport MRT.

## 4. Curated Food & Cafe Hit-List

### Taipei
- **Street Food & Markets:**
  - *Ningxia Night Market:* Liu Yu Zi taro balls (Michelin Bib Gourmand), oyster omelet, pork leg rice.
  - *Raohe Night Market:* Fuzhou Ancestral Pepper Buns (baked in clay tandoors at the entrance).
  - *Breakfast:* Fuhang Soy Milk (savory soy milk curd + *youtiao* crullers) or Yonghe Soy Milk.
- **Cafes & Tea:**
  - *Dadaocheng / Dihua St:* South Street Delight (tea in heritage courtyard), ASW Tea House.
  - *Zhongshan / Chifeng St:* Tella Tella Cafe (retro Japanese-Taiwanese vibe), Melange Cafe.

### Hualien
- **Local Specialties:**
  - *Gongzheng Baozi:* Fluffy steamed mini pork buns.
  - *Dai Ji Wontons (扁食):* Delicate broth with thin-skin pork dumplings.
  - *Dongdamen Market:* Indigenous roasted wild boar (*shan zhu rou*), scallion pancakes (Zhaji).

### Taitung & Chishang
- **Local Specialties:**
  - *Chishang Bento (池上便當):* Served in traditional thin wooden bento boxes featuring premium local valley rice, pork cutlet, braised egg, and pickled greens.
  - *Donghe Steamed Buns:* Iconic roadside pork and red bean steamed buns along the coast.
  - *Aboriginal Flavors:* Millet wine (*xiaomi jiu*), grilled fish with mountain pepper (*maqaw*).

### Kaohsiung
- **Local Specialties:**
  - *Yancheng District Duck Rice (鴨肉珍 - Ya Rou Zhen):* Michelin-recommended braised pork and sliced duck over rice.
  - *Cijin Seafood Street:* Fresh catches grilled or stir-fried right in front of you.
  - *Harbor Cafes:* KW2 (Kaohsiung Port Warehouse No. 2) for seaside coffee overlooking ships.

### Tainan (The Food Capital)
- **Local Specialties:**
  - *Fresh Beef Soup (牛肉湯):* Paper-thin raw local beef instantly flash-cooked by boiling herbal/beef bone broth poured directly into your bowl. Eaten for breakfast! (Try Six Thousand Beef Soup or Wensheng).
  - *Guohua Street Crawl:* *Wa gui* (savory steamed rice cake bowl), crispy shrimp rolls (*Chou's*), Danzai noodles (*Du Xiao Yue*).
  - *Night Cafes & Bars:* Paripari apt. (retro multi-story kissaten cafe & design shop), Bar Home or Moonrock (world-class craft cocktails in renovated old residences).

## 5. Transportation Logistics & Booking Timetable

### 1. Booking Window (Mark Your Calendar)
- **Eastern Coastal Trains (TRA - Taipei → Hualien → Taitung → Kaohsiung):**
  - Tickets open **28 days prior** to travel at 00:00 Taiwan time (UTC+8).
  - *Book via:* Official TRA app (Taiwan Railway) or website. Book express trains (**Taroko Express, Puyuma Express, or EMU3000**) for the fastest, smoothest ride with reserved seats.
- **High-Speed Rail (THSR - Tainan → Taipei):**
  - Tickets open **28 days prior** at 00:00 Taiwan time.
  - Offers "Early Bird" discounts (up to 10%–35% off).

### 2. Local Transit Cards
- **EasyCard (悠遊卡) or iPass (一卡通):**
  - Buy at Taoyuan Airport MRT station or any 7-Eleven / FamilyMart.
  - Tap-to-ride for Taipei MRT, Kaohsiung MRT/Light Rail, local buses, YouBike rentals, and even payments at convenience stores.

### 3. Taxis & Rideshare
- **Uber** works reliably in Taipei, Kaohsiung, and Tainan.
- In Hualien and Taitung, yellow metered taxis are plentiful at train stations, or your hotel/B&B hosts can easily call trusted drivers.

## 6. Practical Tips for November
- **Weather:** November is arguably the best month to visit Taiwan. Temperatures are pleasant (~22°C–27°C / 72°F–80°F), humidity drops, and typhoons are very rare. Evenings can be slightly breezy near the coast.
- **Cash is King for Street Food:** While department stores, high-end restaurants, and hotels accept credit cards, 90% of night market stalls and traditional breakfast spots are strictly cash-only. Modern ATMs at 7-Eleven accept international debit cards with low fees.
